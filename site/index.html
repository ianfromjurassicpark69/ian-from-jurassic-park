Notes on the man

Ian Malcolm is a mathematician who specialises in chaos theory. John Hammond invited him to look over a dinosaur park. He spent the weekend explaining why it would fail. It failed.

Michael Crichton wrote him into the 1990 novel Jurassic Park. Jeff Goldblum played him in the 1993 film and supplied the black shirt, the open collar and the habit of leaning on furniture.

He is the one character in the series whose whole job is to be right in advance.

Fig. 1 The drop test

In the film, Ian puts a drop of water on the back of a hand and asks which way it will roll. He does it again. This time it goes the other way. Same hand, same water, a hair of difference at the start.

Try it. Every drop starts off centre by a different tiny amount, too small to see. Run enough of them and the tally stops looking fair.

Fig. 2 Order, then not

Chaos theory studies systems where small changes at the start produce very different endings. Weather works this way. So does a park built on the assumption that nothing will go wrong.

The picture is the logistic map. The dial r sets how hard the system is pushed. At low settings it settles on one value. Push harder and it splits in two, then four, then it stops repeating at all.
